Best Colours for Real Estate

In short: two proven palette directions for real estate, with typography and usage rules.

Colour is the fastest signal a real estate brand sends. These are the two directions that consistently work — with the reasoning, not just the swatches.

Recommended palettes for Real Estate

1. Royal Heritage — open palette

#14213D#7F1D1D#C9A227#E5D9B6#F8F5EC

Ceremony and confidence — navy, gold and burgundy. Best for: Law firms, universities, luxury goods, finance and formal invitations.

2. Nordic Minimal — open palette

#2D3142#4F5D75#BFC0C0#E8E9EB#FFFFFF

Scandinavian restraint — pale woods, stone and one quiet blue. Best for: Architecture, furniture, premium tech and editorial design.

Typography direction

Trustworthy serif for the brand, plain sans for listings and numbers.

Do / Don't

Do: Photography first: palettes stay neutral so property photos carry the colour.

Don't: Avoid playful brights — buyers read them as risk on the biggest purchase of their lives.

Worked example — a homepage in 20 minutes

Take the first palette: page background in its light neutral, header and footer in the deep base, headings in the deep base on light sections, the primary for section bands, and the accent for exactly two things — the main call-to-action and link hovers. Set body text at 4.5:1 contrast or better (check any pair on its colour page). That skeleton, with the typography direction above, produces a site a client reads as 'real estate' within three seconds — which is precisely what industry colour is for: instant category recognition plus one memorable accent.

Frequently Asked Questions

What colours suit real estate?

Two proven directions are above: Royal Heritage and Nordic Minimal — each with HEX codes, roles and usage rules on its palette page.

Can I mix the two palettes?

Take one as the base and borrow only the other's accent if your brand needs it. Two full palettes at once usually reads as indecision.

Do these colours work in print too?

Yes — every palette page lists roles; per-colour CMYK values for named colours are on their colour pages, and palette HEXes can be converted in the Colour Converter for print proofs.

What matters more — colour or typography?

Colour is recognised first (it is pre-attentive), typography carries the message. This page gives both because either alone underperforms.
