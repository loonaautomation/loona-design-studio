Safe Zone Checker — See What TikTok, Reels & Shorts Cover

In short: drop a still from your vertical video into this checker and it shades exactly what each app's interface will cover — TikTok's buttons and caption, the Reels top and bottom bands, the Shorts title area — and outlines the safe zone your text, face and logo must stay inside. Download the zones as a transparent 1080×1920 PNG overlay for your editor.

Every short-form app draws its own buttons, tabs and captions over your video. Text placed for one app gets buried by another's interface. This checker uses each platform's own published safe-zone numbers, so you can design once and post everywhere with confidence.

Click or tap the frame where your headline, face or logo sits — the readout below tells you whether that spot is safe on the selected platform.

How to use the Safe Zone Checker

  1. Upload a still frame from your 9:16 video — or start with the built-in test frame to see the zones right away.
  2. Pick a platform: TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts — or "All three" for the combined, post-everywhere zone.
  3. Click the spots where your hook text, captions, face and logo sit. Green means visible; red means the app will cover it.
  4. Download the transparent overlay PNG and lay it over the top track of your project in CapCut, Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve or Canva. Design inside the green outline, then hide the overlay layer before exporting.

How it works — the numbers behind the zones

None of the three apps publishes a safe zone for ordinary posts, but all three publish one for vertical ads — and the ad interface sits in the same places as the post interface. This checker draws those published margins on a 1080×1920 frame:

The red bands are deliberately cautious: app layouts shift a little between versions, phones and caption lengths, so a margin that survives every redesign beats one that is exact for a week. Always do a final preview inside the app before a high-stakes post.

A cooking creator puts her title at the very bottom of the frame because it looked clean in her editor. On TikTok it vanished under the caption block — the bottom 660 px. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a safe zone in a vertical video?

It is the part of a 9:16 frame that the app's own interface does not cover — the area where your hook text, captions, face and logo stay fully visible. Everything outside it risks sitting under buttons, the caption block or the top tabs.

Where do these zone numbers come from?

From each platform's own published guidance for vertical ads: Meta's Reels guidance (keep 14% of the top, 35% of the bottom and 6% of each side clear), TikTok's in-feed ad safe-zone file (240 px top, 660 px bottom, 120 px sides, plus the button column) and Google's vertical video guidance for Shorts (288 px top, 672 px bottom, 48 px left, 192 px right) — all on a 1080×1920 frame.

Is my photo uploaded?

Never. The preview, the overlay and the PNG export are all drawn in your browser; your image never leaves your device.

How do I use the overlay PNG in my editor?

Download the transparent overlay, place it on the top track of your 1080×1920 project in CapCut, Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve or Canva, line your text and logo up inside the green outline, then hide or delete the overlay layer before exporting.
